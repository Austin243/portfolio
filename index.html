Austin Ellis

Research intern, Center for Nanophase Materials Sciences,
Oak Ridge National Laboratory

I use first-principles calculations, atomistic simulation, and machine learning to understand how bonding and reactivity shape materials. My projects span superconducting hydrides and electrides under high pressure, defects in 2D materials, and neural networks that predict electronic structure. At Oak Ridge National Laboratory, I’m building automated workflows that use machine-learned interatomic potentials to discover reaction pathways and model their kinetics.

Iron redox chemistry in deep Earth

X. Wang, X. Feng, J. Li, Y. Lv, A. Ellis, S. Scott, A. Pandit, D. Khodagholian, R. J. Hemley, M. G. Jackson, F. J. Spera, S. A. T. Redfern, M. Miao

Accuracy and limits of pretrained interatomic potentials across molecular reaction pathways

A. Ellis, R. Morelock, S. Bagchi, A. M. dos Santos, M. Goswami, J. Jakowski, J. Huang, P. Ganesh, I. Ivanov

Fifteen pretrained interatomic potentials are compared with DFT on 9,017 gas-phase reaction pathways. Models trained on molecular data are the most accurate, and the best, UMA-M OMol, reproduces 96% of 3,713 CHNO barriers within 1 kcal mol−1.

First author.

A practical route to demonstrating core reactivity in Cs polyoxides under pressure

Y. Sun, S. Ellis, D. Khodagholian, L. Wyke, A. Ellis, P. C. Müller, B. N. Schüpp, R. Nelson, R. Dronskowski, M. Miao

First-principles structure searches show that the polyoxygen units of Cs–O compounds break down under pressure as the Cs core electrons begin to participate in bonding.

Core reactivity in Cs–Ba–O ternary system under pressure

S. Ellis, A. Ellis, L. Wyke, C. Taylor, M. Miao

High pressure can bring the Cs 5p core electrons into covalent bonding. Structure searches in the Cs–Ba–O system at 150 and 250 GPa find CsBa3O8 and CsBaO4 on the convex hull at 250 GPa, with weak Cs 5p–O 2p bonding and intact O2 units. Cs2BaO3 shows stronger bonding but is metastable.

2nd of 5 authors.

ChemRxiv 2026 Preprint

WISP: learning spin-resolved electron localization from atomic environments

A. Ellis

A network that predicts the electron localization function of both spin channels at any point in a crystal, from the atomic structure and the magnetic moments on the atoms. On 21,922 held-out structures the mean absolute error is 0.011 per channel on the ELF scale of 0 to 1.

Sole author.

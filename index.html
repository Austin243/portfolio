I use first-principles calculations, atomistic simulation, and machine learning to understand how bonding and reactivity shape materials. My projects span superconducting hydrides and electrides under high pressure, defects in 2D materials, and neural networks that predict electronic structure. At Oak Ridge National Laboratory, I’m building automated workflows that use machine-learned interatomic potentials to discover reaction pathways and model their kinetics.

jobs/elfnet J. Mater. Inf. 2026 · NeurIPS AI4Mat 2025 · APS 2026

ELFNet: electron localization from superposed atomic densities

A. Ellis, S. Ellis, M. Miao

my roleFirst author. I designed, trained, and tested the network.

A periodic 3D convolutional network maps inexpensive superposed atomic densities to DFT electron localization functions, so bonding and interstitial localization can be screened before a full calculation.

jobs/superhydrides J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2025 · NeurIPS AI4Mat 2025

Metal superhydrides from chemical templates

Y. Sun, A. Ellis, X. Chen, M. Miao

my role2nd of 4 authors. I helped build the training set and ran the template-guided CALYPSO searches and stability screening.

A template-guided machine-learning workflow found 13 new structural prototypes and 31 stable metal superhydrides, 19 of them with predicted Tc above 100 K.

paperworkshopposteropen U10H84_I4m/

jobs/h2-electride J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 2026

Emerging electride in molecular hydrogen metallization

A. Ellis, S. Ellis, A. Pandit, M. Miao

my roleFirst author. I ran the PBE and HSE06 calculations and the analysis of the interstitial states.

As solid hydrogen metallizes under pressure, electrons localize in the interstitial regions between H2 molecules, an electride-like route that sets it apart from other molecular crystals.

paperthesis slidesopen H2_states/

jobs/tmdc-electrides J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 2024

Tunable electrides on monolayer TMDCs

Y. Sun, A. Ellis, S. Diaz, W. Li, M. Miao

my role2nd of 5 authors. I analyzed the vacancy states with ELF, charge-density differences, and band structures, and helped with the H-adsorption calculations.

Chalcogen vacancies strengthen the weak electride character of monolayer transition-metal dichalcogenides to the level of known electrides, and the vacancy states help catalyze hydrogen evolution.

W. Zhao, A. Ellis, D. Duan, H. Wang, Q. Jiang, M. Du, T. Cui, M. Miao

jobs/fe-redox Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. 2025

Iron redox chemistry in deep Earth

X. Wang, X. Feng, J. Li, Y. Lv, A. Ellis, S. Scott, A. Pandit, D. Khodagholian, R. J. Hemley, M. G. Jackson, F. J. Spera, S. A. T. Redfern, M. Miao

jobs/wisp ChemRxiv 2026 · preprint

WISP: learning spin-resolved electron localization from atomic environments

A. Ellis

my roleSole author.

A network that predicts the electron localization function of both spin channels at any point in a crystal, from the atomic structure and the magnetic moments on the atoms. On 21,922 held-out structures the mean absolute error is 0.011 per channel on the ELF scale of 0 to 1.

preprintopen MnPd/open Sc5Cl8/

A. Ellis, S. Bagchi, M. Goswami, J. Jakowski, J. Huang, P. Ganesh, I. Ivanov

jobs/cs-core-reactivity arXiv 2026 · preprint

Core reactivity in Cs–Ba–O ternary system under pressure

S. Ellis, A. Ellis, L. Wyke, C. Taylor, M. Miao

my role2nd of 5 authors.

High pressure can bring the Cs 5p core electrons into covalent bonding. Structure searches in the Cs–Ba–O system at 150 and 250 GPa put CsBa3O8 and CsBaO4 on the convex hull at 250 GPa, with mild Cs 5p–O 2p bonding and O2 units still intact. Cs2BaO3 bonds more strongly but is metastable.

A MoS2 monolayer with one sulfur removed at the center of the flake. Every S carries a lone-pair ELF basin; at the vacancy a smaller basin with no atom inside sits where the sulfur was: the electride-like vacancy state.

C2/c-12 solid hydrogen, band 15 at Γ (Fig. 3a of the paper). The lowest conduction state runs in continuous channels between the H2 molecules: electrons moving into the interstitial space as the gap closes.

Ama2 hydrogen at 425 GPa, band 25 at R (Fig. 3b). Compression pushes this state out of the H–H bonds and into the interstitial space between the molecular layers.

P63/m hydrogen, band 9 at A (Fig. 3c): interstitial maxima stacked in columns along c, between the H2 molecules.

P63/m hydrogen, band 11 at A (Fig. 3d): the next state up also gathers in the interstitial pockets instead of on the H–H bonds.

A TaS2 sheet with one sulfur missing from the lower layer, seen from below. The sulfur above the gap has sunk toward the tantalum plane.
